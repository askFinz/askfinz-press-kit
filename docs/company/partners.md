## Key points

### Three ways this usually starts

A partnership here usually starts in one of three ways. If none of them is quite you, say so on the form — the interesting ones rarely fit a category.

### When not to write

You just want a site read. You do not need a partnership for that.

### Specific things, not a relationship

Read when you publish Pages become answerable as they are read rather than after a rebuild, so a correction you make today is what a reader gets today.

### For the material only you hold

Plenty of what an organisation knows is not on a page anyone can reach. It sits in internal documentation, product manuals, corporate sites behind a staging domain, and the knowledge clusters a team has built up over years.

### One appliance, two kinds of shelf

Indexer OS does not only feed the public index. The same machine can build collections that are yours alone — and a collection is a shelf, so it can be split the way your organisation is split.

### A collection is not a filing cabinet

Contributing is not archiving. Once material is on a shelf it is read from the workspaces people already work in — a question in Research, a company view in Finance, a course in Learn — so the answer arrives inside the task rather than being something somebody has to go and look up first.

### Four steps, and one of them is ours

A couple of sentences: what you publish or keep, and what you would want read.

### Tell us what you hold

There is no form-filling exercise behind this and no sales sequence. It sends one email to a mailbox a person reads, and you get a reply from that person.
